# Market sizing — Mumbai diaspora in the 5 shipping countries

Prepared 25 November 2026. Figures below are a mix of **measured data** (cited)
and **derived estimates** (assumptions shown). Every estimate is labelled.
Nothing here is a forecast.

---

## 1. The honest problem with the question

"How many Mumbai people live in these 5 countries" has **no measured answer.**
No census in the US, Canada, UAE, UK or Singapore asks which Indian city a
person came from. India does not publish emigration by city either — the
Ministry of External Affairs only tracks ECR-passport workers to 18 ECR
countries, which excludes most emigration to these five destinations.

So this has to be derived from what *is* measured: total Indian diaspora per
country, plus language data as a partial proxy. The derivation is below, with
its error bars stated.

---

| UAE | 17,760 | 4,326,248 | **4,344,008** |

Source: [MEA, Population of Overseas Indians](https://www.mea.gov.in/population-of-overseas-indians)

**So ~15.4 million Indians live in your five shipping countries.** That part is
solid. Note the MEA page carries a second, undated table with materially
different numbers; the figures above are from the dated January 2026 table.

## 3. Derived: how many are Mumbai-origin

The only hard proxy is Marathi language data:

| Country | Marathi speakers | Source |
|---|---:|---|
| USA | ~107,300 | [ACS 2017–21 detailed languages](https://settleinamerica.com/guides/indian/marathi/) |
| England | 10,865 | [Census 2021, TS024](https://censusdata.uk/e92000001-england/ts024-main-language-detailed) |
| Canada | ~8,295 | [2016 Census](https://www12.statcan.gc.ca/census-recensement/2016/dp-pd/prof/details/page.cfm?Lang=E&Geo1=PR&Code1=01&Geo2=PR&Code2=01&Data=Count&SearchText=canada&SearchType=Begins&SearchPR=01&B1=Language&TABID=1) |
| UAE, Singapore | not published | — |

In the US that is **1.8% of the Indian diaspora** — the cleanest ratio
available, since both numbers are recent and from the same country.

Marathi speakers are **not** the same as Mumbai-origin, in both directions:

- **Understates it.** Mumbai is only about 45% Marathi-speaking. Gujarati,
  Hindi, Urdu, Tamil and Telugu-speaking Mumbaikars are just as likely to buy
  Mumbai farsan — arguably more so, since Gujarati families are a core
  customer for this exact product category.
- **Understates it again.** Second-generation migrants usually report English
  as their main language, so they vanish from these counts entirely.
- **Overstates it.** Marathi speakers also come from Pune, Nashik, Nagpur and
  rural Maharashtra, not just Mumbai.

Working the US ratio through: if Marathi diaspora is 1.8%, and roughly 60% of
Marathi emigrants are from Mumbai/MMR, and Mumbai is ~45% Marathi, then
Mumbai-origin ≈ 1.8% × 0.6 ÷ 0.45 ≈ **2.4%** of the Indian diaspora.

Cross-check: Mumbai metro is ~1.5% of India's population, but metro, English-
educated, passport-holding populations emigrate at several times the national
rate. 2–4% is consistent with that. Against it: Maharashtra ranked only 8th
among states for emigration clearances in 2003–2012 data, so the state as a
whole is an under-emigrator.

**Estimate — Mumbai-origin people in the five countries:**

| | Share of diaspora | People | Households (÷3) |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Low | 2.0% | 309,000 | 103,000 |
| **Mid** | **3.0%** | **463,000** | **154,000** |
| High | 4.0% | 617,000 | 206,000 |

**Confidence: low.** This is a derived range, not a count. Treat 300,000–600,000
as the honest width, and don't quote the mid-point as if it were measured.

## 4. How many actually buy this kind of product

Qualitatively strong, quantitatively thin. No survey measures what diaspora
households spend on Indian groceries — I looked.

What is known:

- Indian grocery stores were estimated to capture **60–70% of Indian
  households' grocery spend** in the US ([Forbes, 2019](https://www.forbes.com/sites/brittainladd/2019/01/30/lifestyle-brand-and-retailer-subziwalla-wants-to-be-the-amazon-of-indian-groceries-in-the-u.s./)) — dated, but
  the direction is clear.
- Patel Brothers alone passed **$1bn annual revenue** by end-2023 across ~51
  US stores.
- India's snacks exports grew **$0.54bn (2019) → $1.2bn (2024)**, with the US
  at 23.9% and UAE at 10% of that ([TPCI](https://www.indiabusinesstrade.in/blogs/indias-snack-industry/)).
- Indian-American households earn **over twice** the US average
  ([Kellogg Insight](https://insight.kellogg.northwestern.edu/article/south-asian-retail)) — spending power is not the constraint.

So: nearly all of these households already buy Indian snacks. The constraint is
not demand for namkeen — it is **why they would buy yours, shipped from India,
instead of walking into the Indian store they already use.**

That is the number that matters, and it is a marketing question, not a
market-size question.

## 5. Derived: year-one revenue, Mumbai-origin customers only

Assumptions, all changeable:

- Serviceable households: **154,000** (mid estimate above)
- Average order: **4 kg mixed, ≈ ₹3,500** product value
  (freight makes 1 kg orders uneconomic — see §6)
- Orders per buying household per year: **2–3** (festival-led, not weekly)

| Scenario | Year-1 penetration | Buying households | Orders | Revenue |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| Conservative | 0.5% | 770 | 1,540 | **₹54 lakh** |
| Mid | 1.0% | 1,540 | 3,850 | **₹1.35 crore** |
| Optimistic | 2.0% | 3,080 | 9,240 | **₹3.23 crore** |

**Read the conservative row as the planning number.** A new brand with no
recognition, ordering only through WhatsApp, with no paid marketing, reaching
even 0.5% of a diaspora segment in year one would be a good result. Most
reach zero because they never solve distribution.

Also: penetration here is of the *Mumbai-origin* segment only, as asked. The
product has no reason to stop there — any Indian household abroad is a
candidate, which is 15.4M people rather than 463,000. Restricting to Mumbai
origin shrinks the market ~33× for no commercial reason.

---

## 6. The constraint that outranks all of the above

Shelf life versus freight:

- Namkeen fried in oil goes rancid in roughly **2–3 months**.
- Sea freight India → US/UK is **4–6 weeks** plus customs. That consumes half
  the shelf life before it reaches a shelf.
- So **air freight is effectively mandatory**, and air freight on food is
  roughly **₹1,200–2,000 per kg** to North America.

At ₹600/kg product and ₹1,500/kg freight, **freight is 70% of landed cost.**
This is the whole business problem. It is why orders must be 3–5 kg, and why
discounting the product is the wrong lever.

Before any of this matters, the regulatory floor:

- **US** — FDA Food Facility Registration + Prior Notice on every shipment
- **UAE** — halal certification and Arabic labelling
- **UK/Canada** — allergen and nutrition labelling to local format
- **All** — FSSAI export registration at the India end

Selling 1 kg of namkeen to a friend abroad is informal. Selling it as a
business to five countries is a regulated food export.

---

## 7. Margin recommendation

**Target 50–55% gross margin on product.** Floor at 35% — below that, freight
variance and 2–5% spoilage/returns will take the rest.

**Do not launch with a price discount.** Two reasons:

1. It anchors the product low, and raising prices later costs you customers
   you acquired *because* of the price.
2. The customer's real objection is not the ₹600 — it is the ₹1,500 freight
   sitting next to it.

**Subsidise freight instead.** Options, in order of preference:

| Lever | Why it works |
|---|---|
| Flat shipping above 3 kg | Raises order size, which is exactly what amortises freight |
| Free shipping above ₹4,000 | Same effect, framed as a reward |
| Festival bundles (5 kg assorted) | Sells the weight you need without discounting the kilo |
| 10–15% off first order only, capped | Acquisition cost with a known ceiling |

A flat-shipping threshold that pushes a 1 kg order to 4 kg earns you more than
a 20% product discount costs you, and it leaves your price list intact.

**First 90 days:** price at full margin, give away freight on larger baskets,
and spend the difference on getting the first 50 customers to reorder. Reorder
rate is the only number that tells you whether this is a business.

## 8. Domestic — Mumbai-origin in other Indian cities

Better measured than the diaspora, because the Census records mother tongue by
city. Native Marathi speakers outside Maharashtra, Census 2011:

| City | Native Marathi speakers | % of city |
|---|---:|---:|
| Surat | 447,000 | 10.0% |
| **Bengaluru** | **175,000** | 2.1% |
| Belgaum | 161,000 | 32.9% |
| Vadodara | 133,000 | 7.6% |
| Ahmedabad | 113,000 | 2.0% |
| Indore | 103,500 | 5.2% |
| **Hyderabad** | **102,000** | 1.5% |
| Delhi (whole NCT) | ~27,000 | 0.2% |

Source: [Census 2011 city figures](https://x.com/BharadiaSamarth/status/2095821841281757663),
[Marathi speakers by state](https://themapsdaily.com/posts/in-marathi-speakers)

**Most of these are not migrants.** Belgaum, Surat, Vadodara, Ahmedabad and
Indore have had settled Marathi communities for generations — border history,
not people who left Mumbai. They are Marathi, but they are not *Mumbai*
customers with a Mumbai palate and Mumbai nostalgia.

The migration-driven cities are **Bengaluru, Hyderabad and Delhi NCR** — the
IT and corporate-transfer destinations.

**Estimate — Mumbai-origin people living outside Maharashtra:**

| City | Mumbai-origin estimate | Basis |
|---|---:|---|
| Bengaluru | ~120,000 | 175k Marathi × ~50% Mumbai/MMR, grossed up for non-Marathi Mumbaikars |
| Delhi NCR | ~50,000 | corporate transfers; Marathi count understates badly here |
| Hyderabad | ~55,000 | 102k × ~40%, same gross-up |
| Chennai, Kolkata, Goa, Pune-adjacent | ~80,000 | residual |
| **Total** | **~300,000–400,000** | |

Cross-check: Census 2011 put total Maharashtra out-migrants at about
**1,138,753** on a place-of-birth basis. Mumbai/MMR would be 25–35% of that —
285k to 400k — which lands in the same place. ([IJAR overview of 2011 migration tables](https://www.worldwidejournals.com/indian-journal-of-applied-research-(IJAR)/recent_issues_pdf/2020/May/an-overview-of-trends-and-patterns-of-internal-migration-in-india-2011_May_2020_1588325627_9408784.pdf))

**So domestic Mumbai-origin (~350k) is slightly smaller than international
Mumbai-origin (~463k).** On headcount alone, domestic looks like the weaker
market. On economics it is dramatically the stronger one.

## 9. Why domestic is the better business

| | Domestic (Bengaluru/Delhi) | Export (US/UK/UAE) |
|---|---|---|
| Freight per kg | ₹60–100 surface | ₹1,200–2,000 air |
| Freight as share of landed cost | **~12%** | **~70%** |
| Transit time | 2–4 days | 5–10 days air, 4–6 weeks sea |
| Shelf life risk | negligible | severe — 2–3 month rancidity window |
| Regulation | FSSAI only | FSSAI + FDA / halal / local labelling |
| Payments | UPI, instant | FX, gateway fees, chargebacks |
| Replacing a damaged order | ₹100 | ₹1,500 |

**Order economics, per customer per year:**

| | Domestic | Export |
|---|---:|---:|
| Average order | 2 kg, ~₹1,300 | 4 kg, ~₹3,500 |
| Orders/year | 4–6 | 2–3 |
| Gross margin/order | ~₹650 | ~₹1,800 |
| **Annual margin/customer** | **₹2,600–3,900** | **₹3,600–5,400** |

Export wins per customer. Domestic wins on everything that decides whether you
get the customer at all: lower price point to try, no shipping sticker shock,
faster delivery, cheaper mistakes, no regulatory gate.

**Year-1 domestic revenue**, Mumbai-origin households in the three real cities
(~225,000 people ÷ 3.5 ≈ 64,000 households):

| Scenario | Penetration | Households | Revenue |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| Conservative | 1% | 640 | **₹33 lakh** |
| Mid | 2% | 1,280 | **₹67 lakh** |
| Optimistic | 4% | 2,560 | **₹1.66 crore** |

Penetration assumptions are 2× the export ones because acquisition is far
easier at a ₹1,400 order than a ₹9,500 one.

## 10. The Mumbai filter is the wrong target domestically

Worth saying plainly: inside India, targeting Mumbai-origin customers throws
away most of the market for no reason.

- India's savoury snacks market is about **₹46,571 crore** (IMARC, 2024).
- Mumbai farsan is already a recognised **premium regional category**
  nationwide — the same way Bikaneri bhujia travels from Bikaner and Haldiram's
  from Nagpur. Nobody checks whether the buyer is from there.
- A Kannadiga family in Bengaluru buying good chakli is a customer. A Punjabi
  family in Gurgaon buying bhakarwadi is a customer.

Mumbai origin is useful as a **first beachhead** — those 120,000 in Bengaluru
are the cheapest people to convert, because they already know what Bhavnagari
gathiya should taste like and currently cannot get it. Land them, use them for
word of mouth, then widen.

Do not build the business on the filter. Build it on the beachhead.
